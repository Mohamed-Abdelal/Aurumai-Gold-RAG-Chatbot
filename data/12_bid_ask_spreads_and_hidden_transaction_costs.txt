Title: Bid-Ask Spreads and Hidden Transaction Costs in Gold
Category: Risks, Costs & 2026 Market
Date: 2026-11-15
Source: AurumAI Risk Analysis

One of the most underestimated drags on gold investment returns is the **round-trip transaction cost**—the combined gap between what you pay to acquire gold and what you receive if you sell it back on the very same day.

Three components make up total transaction friction:
1. **Dealer Bid-Ask Spread:** The difference between the dealer's selling price (ask) and buying price (bid) for raw gold weight. For liquid Gold ETFs (like GLDM or IAU), the bid-ask spread is minuscule at 0.02% to 0.08%. For local physical bullion dealers, the raw spot bid-ask spread typically runs 0.5% to 1.5% in calm markets, but can widen to 2% to 4% during extreme volatility or rapid currency swings.
2. **Fabrication Premium / Making Charge (Masna'eya):** Physical bars and coins carry an upfront minting, refining, assay, and packaging fee. Because fixed minting costs are spread over fewer grams, small bars suffer steeply higher percentage markups: a 1-gram bar often costs 6% to 12% over spot, a 5-gram bar 3.5% to 5.5%, a 10-gram bar 2.5% to 4%, and a 50g or 100g bar just 1.5% to 2.5%. Fashion jewelry is worst of all for investing, with 15% to 35% non-recoverable workmanship markups.
3. **Fund Management & Early-Redemption Fees:** Spot Gold ETFs charge 0.10% to 0.40% per year with no exit penalty. Local gold mutual funds (such as Egypt's AZ-Gold) charge an annual management/custody fee (around 1% total expense ratio) plus a sliding early-redemption fee (typically starting around 4.9% in year 1 and declining to 0% after 3 full years) specifically designed to discourage short-term speculation.

**Break-Even Holding Period Rule:** If your combined upfront making charge plus bid-ask spread equals 4% to 6%, gold must rise by at least 4% to 6% merely for you to break even. Never use high-spread physical bullion or 3-year-vesting gold mutual funds for short-term trading or 3-to-9-month cash parking.
